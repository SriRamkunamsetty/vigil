# Responsible AI: model card and safeguards

## Summary

Vigil helps a person decide, in the moment, whether a call and a payment together look like a scam. It adds friction in proportion to the risk. It is a research prototype, evaluated only on synthetic data, and not ready to protect real money without the steps in "Before real use".

## Intended use

* Warn a customer during a suspicious call or payment.
* Help a bank analyst review linked accounts that pass money along.
* Check purchases made by an AI agent against what the customer authorised.

## Out of scope

* Deciding on its own to freeze an account, accuse a person, or report to the police.
* Scoring people for credit, insurance or employment.
* Use on real customer data before the validation below.

## How decisions are made

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Who decides the tier? | A deterministic additive model (`risk/model.py`). No language model is in that path. |
| What does Gemini do? | Finds tactics in paraphrased wording and rewrites the warning more simply. It can add concern, never remove it. |
| Can it be explained? | Yes. Every score returns each feature's contribution and the quoted phrases, in English, Hindi or Telugu. |
| Can a human override it? | The customer always can. A hold ends in a bank call-back, never a final refusal. |
| Where are the weights from? | Set by hand as priors and tuned on a development set. They are not fitted to real fraud. A prior-anchored fitting routine and a calibration check are included for when real labels exist. |

## Data

* **Training data:** none. The model has no learned parameters from customer data.
* **Evaluation data:** synthetic, generated by `synthetic/`. See `EVALUATION.md` for how and for the limits.
* **At run time:** transcripts and payment context are processed per request. Sessions are in memory with a time limit and can be erased on request. Audit logs record the tier, score and scam type, not the conversation, payee or amount.

## Safeguards built in

| Risk | Safeguard |
| --- | --- |
| Personal data reaching a model | Redaction of UPI IDs, phones, PAN, Aadhaar, cards, account numbers, emails and OTPs before any LLM call. |
| Prompt injection from a scammer | Transcript is delimited and declared untrusted; model output is schema-checked and quotes must appear in the transcript; injection attempts raise risk; the model cannot lower a tier. |
| Model writes something harmful | Composer validates language script, rejects invented numbers or links and "safe to pay" phrasing, and always keeps the official helpline step. |
| Over-blocking honest people | Ten kinds of hard-negative situations are tested; friction levels are graded; holds are reversible; the UI shows where friction still happens too often. |
| Over-protecting or patronising | Extra protection for seniors is opt-in and only lowers thresholds. |
| Harm to third parties by bad reports | A payee counts as confirmed only after two banks report it; signals expire, can be retracted and are rate-limited per bank; publishing needs a named human approval. |
| Agent misuse | `require_confirmation` on actions that affect other people; agents act only through tools. |
| Outages | Fails open to the deterministic path with templates; `ai_layer` is reported. |

## Known limitations and biases

* **Paraphrased scams are the main weakness.** On the synthetic set, recall for unfamiliar wording is less than half that for familiar wording. The Gemini layer is meant to help, but it has not been measured.
* **Language coverage is uneven.** Hindi, Telugu and romanised mixes are covered by hand-written patterns. Other Indian languages, dialects, code-switching and speech-to-text errors are not.
* **Honest edge cases get friction.** Large first-time payments under time pressure, such as buying a used car or a real family emergency, are flagged more often than average.
* **Warning text is unreviewed.** Hindi and Telugu messages were written for this prototype and need review by native speakers and, ideally, usability tests with first-time smartphone users.
* **Scores are not probabilities.** Hand-set weights give a useful ranking, not a calibrated likelihood.
* **Age is a blunt proxy.** The senior option exists because of who scammers target, but it must be opt-in and never used to restrict a person's own money.
* **Fairness has not been tested.** There is no real data to test differences across regions, languages, incomes or ages.

## Before real use

1. Review all Hindi and Telugu text with native speakers; test with people in the target group.
2. Collect labelled, consented, real outcomes with the bank's fraud team and re-fit and re-calibrate the weights; measure precision at realistic prevalence.
3. Measure the Gemini layer on a labelled multilingual paraphrase set; pin and review model versions.
4. Test fairness across language, region, age and income groups.
5. Complete the bank's own model-risk, security and privacy reviews, a data protection impact assessment, and legal review of consent, retention and notification flows.
6. Run in shadow mode (warn nobody, log decisions) before any customer-facing friction.
7. Set up monitoring for drift, false-alarm complaints and appeals, with a named owner.

## Regulatory context

Vigil was designed with these in mind: India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (purpose limitation, minimisation, consent, erasure) and the Reserve Bank of India's work on responsible AI in finance (human oversight, explainability, accountability, grievance redress). Design choices that follow from them are redaction, no stored transcripts in audit logs, session erasure, opt-in protections and human approval for consequential actions. **This is design intent, not legal advice or a compliance certification.** Check current rules, which change, with the bank's compliance team.

## Contact and appeals

A customer who is held should be able to reach a human. The intervention always names the bank call-back, the 1930 helpline and cybercrime.gov.in. A published bank-to-bank signal can be retracted by the bank that reported it.
